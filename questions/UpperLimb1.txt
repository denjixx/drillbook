Q: Which one of the following statements about the clavicle is correct?
A) It is the last long bone of the upper limb to begin ossification
B) It has a well-developed medullary cavity in its shaft
C) It is a long bone that lies vertically and is deeply covered by muscles
D) It is the only long bone that ossifies in membrane (except its medial end) and the first bone in the body to start ossifying
E) It ossifies from one primary and two secondary centres
Answer: D
Explanation: The clavicle is the first bone to ossify, ossifies in membrane (except the medial end), has two primary centres (5th-6th week of intrauterine life) and one secondary centre for the medial end, lies horizontally, is subcutaneous throughout and has no medullary cavity. [Chaurasia]

Q: A 50-year-old man falls off his bicycle, hears a crack and notes that the lateral part of his shoulder droops while the medial end of the clavicle appears elevated. Where is the fracture most likely located and why does the shoulder droop?
A) Medial end near the sternoclavicular joint; the costoclavicular ligament is torn
B) Junction of the medial two-thirds and lateral one-third; the lateral fragment is pulled down by the pectoralis major
C) Middle of the shaft at the subclavian groove; the subclavius pulls the fragment down
D) Lateral end at the acromioclavicular joint; the deltoid pulls the fragment down
E) Junction of the medial two-thirds and lateral one-third; the unsupported lateral fragment is pulled down by the weight of the limb
Answer: E
Explanation: The clavicle usually fractures at the junction of its two curvatures (medial two-thirds and lateral one-third), the weakest point, lying between the coracoclavicular and costoclavicular ligaments. The trapezius alone cannot support the weight of the upper limb, so the lateral fragment drops, while the medial fragment is elevated by the sternocleidomastoid. [Chaurasia]

B) Levator C5; rhomboids C3
C) Both T1
D) Levator C8, T1; rhomboids C6
E) Levator scapulae C3, 4, 5; rhomboids C4, 5
Answer: E
Explanation: Dorsal scapular nerve with C3, C4 for the levator. [Snell]

Q: Which root values supply latissimus dorsi per Snell's table?
A) C8, T1 via ulnar
B) C6, 7, 8 via thoracodorsal nerve
C) C1, 2 via cervical
D) C5 via dorsal scapular
E) C3, 4 via accessory
Answer: B
Explanation: It extends, adducts and medially rotates the arm. [Snell]

Q: What are the pectoralis major roots?
A) T2 only
B) C5, 6, 7, 8 and T1
C) C5 only
D) C7 only
E) C3, 4
Answer: B
Explanation: Nerve: medial and lateral pectoral. [Snell]

Q: Which root values supply the serratus anterior per Snell?
A) C1, 2
B) C5, 6, 7
C) C8, T1
D) T2
E) C3, 4
Answer: B
Explanation: Long thoracic nerve. [Snell]

Q: Which root values supply the biceps brachii and the brachialis per Snell?
A) C3, C4
B) C7, C8
C) C5, C6 via the musculocutaneous nerve
D) C8, T1
E) T1, T2
Answer: C
Explanation: Coracobrachialis is C5, 6, 7. [Snell]
