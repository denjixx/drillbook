Q: A 65-year-old woman falls on her outstretched hand and has a 'dinner-fork' deformity of the wrist. Which finding is expected on examination?
A) The radial styloid lies at the same level as or proximal to the ulnar styloid
B) The radial styloid lies 2 cm proximal to the ulnar styloid
C) The scaphoid is displaced anteriorly
D) The radial styloid lies about 1 cm distal to the ulnar styloid, as normal
E) The distal radial fragment is displaced anteriorly
Answer: A
Explanation: In Colles' fracture (about 2 cm above the lower end of the radius) the distal fragment is displaced upwards and backwards, so the radial styloid comes to lie proximal to the ulnar styloid (normally it lies about 1 cm distal). Anterior displacement is called Smith's fracture. [Chaurasia]
