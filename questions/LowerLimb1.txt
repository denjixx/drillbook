E) Rigid flatfoot due to tarsal coalition
Answer: C
Explanation: A flexible flatfoot has an arch that reappears on tiptoe and is benign in children. A rigid flatfoot suggests tarsal coalition, while in adults posterior tibial tendon dysfunction is the leading cause of acquired flatfoot.

Q: A 55-year-old woman has progressive loss of the medial arch with the forefoot abducted. Which is the leading cause in adults?
A) Tarsal coalition
B) Plantar fasciitis
C) Hallux valgus
D) Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease
E) Posterior tibial tendon dysfunction
Answer: E
Explanation: In adults, posterior tibial tendon dysfunction is the leading cause of pes planus. Tibialis posterior slips support the medial arch.

Q: A teenager has an abnormally high medial arch with claw toes and a family history of neuropathy. What should be looked for?
A) Tarsal tunnel syndrome
B) Rickets
C) Plantar fasciitis
D) Fracture of the talus
E) Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease or other neurological cause
Answer: E
Explanation: Pes cavus results from muscle imbalance; the usual neurological cause is Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, though spinal cord lesions and poliomyelitis are also causes.

Q: A distance runner has pain posterior to the heel and a prominence on the calcaneus. Which bursa is involved?

C) Prepatellar bursa
D) Ischial bursa

Answer: E
Explanation: Retrocalcaneal bursitis ("long-distance runner's ankle") is often seen with insertional Achilles tendinopathy and a Haglund deformity.

Q: Loss of push-off, as in rupture of tendo calcaneus or tibial nerve palsy, produces which gait?
A) Trendelenburg gait
B) High-stepping gait
C) Scissor gait
D) Waddling gait
E) Flat-footed gait
Answer: E
Explanation: Push-off depends on gastrocnemius, soleus and plantaris plantar flexing the ankle and the long and short flexors flexing the toes. Their loss produces a flat-footed gait.

Q: Which statement describes the course of the flexor digitorum longus tendon in the sole?

Explanation: FDL goes behind the medial malleolus, across the sustentaculum, crosses FHL (receiving a slip), is joined by quadratus plantae and splits into four tendons giving origin to lumbricals before perforating FDB to reach the distal phalanges.

Q: The tendon of which muscle runs in the groove under the cuboid, roofed by the long plantar ligament, to insert on the 1st metatarsal base and medial cuneiform?

Q: Which structure passes both superficial to the extensor retinacula and is accompanied by the great saphenous vein at the front of the ankle?

Q: A 45-year-old obese man complains of burning pain and numbness over the lateral thigh without weakness. Which nerve is most likely compressed as it enters the thigh behind the lateral end of the inguinal ligament?
A) Femoral nerve

Explanation: The lateral (femoral) cutaneous nerve of thigh (L2, L3) enters the thigh behind the lateral end of the inguinal ligament and supplies the lateral thigh; compression produces meralgia paresthetica with sensory symptoms only, since the nerve is purely cutaneous.

Q: A nurse prepares to give an intramuscular injection at the ventrogluteal site, placing the palm over the greater trochanter. Which muscle receives the injection?

Q: Which three muscles share a common insertion at the pes anserinus on the upper medial tibia?
A) Gracilis, adductor longus, biceps femoris

D) Sartorius, gracilis, semimembranosus

Q: The obturator nerve (L2, L3, L4) leaves the pelvis through which opening?
A) Femoral canal
B) Obturator canal
C) Adductor hiatus
D) Lesser sciatic foramen
E) Greater sciatic foramen
Answer: B
Explanation: The obturator nerve passes through the obturator canal, divides into anterior and posterior divisions, and supplies the adductors, hip and knee joints and a variable patch of skin of the medial thigh. The obturator artery arises from the internal iliac artery.

Q: A patient has a stab wound at the inguinal region. The femoral nerve is divided about 4 cm below the inguinal ligament. Which of the following is most likely lost?
A) Hip adduction
B) Eversion of the foot
C) Plantar flexion
D) Knee extension
E) Hip abduction
Answer: D
Explanation: The femoral nerve supplies the quadriceps femoris (knee extension), sartorius and pectineus, and gives the saphenous nerve. Hip adduction depends on the obturator nerve, and hip abduction on the superior gluteal nerve.

Q: Which muscle of the lateral compartment of the leg is supplied by branches of the fibular artery and which nerve?
