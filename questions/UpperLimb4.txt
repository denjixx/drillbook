Q: Which structure on the radius provides the insertion for the biceps brachii tendon?
A) Styloid process
B) Radial notch
C) Coronoid process
D) Ulnar tuberosity
E) Radial tuberosity
Answer: E
Explanation: The radial tuberosity is the oblong prominence just distal to the radial neck and receives the biceps brachii tendon. The ulnar tuberosity and coronoid process (ulna) receive brachialis, the styloid process receives brachioradialis, and the radial notch is on the ulna and receives the radial head.

Q: In situ, how does the radial styloid process compare with the ulnar styloid process?
A) About 1 cm more distal
B) About 3 cm more distal
C) At exactly the same level
D) About 3 cm more proximal
E) About 1 cm more proximal
Answer: A
Explanation: The radius normally extends about 1 cm further distally than the ulna, and its styloid process gives insertion to brachioradialis. Loss of this relationship is a subtle sign of a shortened distal radius.

Q: Which carpal bone is a sesamoid bone contained within the tendon of flexor carpi ulnaris?
A) Scaphoid
B) Hamate
C) Pisiform
D) Trapezium
E) Capitate
Answer: C
Explanation: The pisiform sits within the flexor carpi ulnaris tendon and is the one proximal-row carpal that does not articulate with the radius or articular disk.

Q: What is the correct lateral-to-medial order of the proximal row of carpal bones?
A) Scaphoid, triquetrum, lunate, pisiform
B) Lunate, scaphoid, pisiform, triquetrum
C) Pisiform, triquetrum, lunate, scaphoid
D) Scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform
E) Trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate
Answer: D
Explanation: The proximal row from lateral to medial is scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform. The last option is the distal row.

Q: Which carpal bone is the most lateral of the distal row?
A) Capitate
B) Trapezoid
C) Scaphoid
D) Hamate
E) Trapezium
Answer: E
Explanation: The distal row from lateral to medial is trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate. The scaphoid is the most lateral bone of the proximal row.

Q: With which structure does the head (distal end) of the ulna articulate at the wrist region?
A) Articular disk of the distal radioulnar joint
B) Hamate
C) Capitate
D) Lunate
E) Scaphoid
Answer: A
Explanation: The ulna has no direct contact with the carpal bones; its head articulates with the articular disk, and it is connected to the carpus only by minor ligaments.

Q: Which feature of the ulna accommodates the head of the radius at the proximal radioulnar joint?
A) Ulnar tuberosity
B) Radial notch
C) Trochlear notch
D) Styloid process
E) Olecranon
Answer: B

Q: Which ulnar feature provides insertion for the triceps tendon?
A) Radial notch
B) Coronoid process
C) Styloid process
D) Olecranon
E) Ulnar tuberosity
Answer: D
Explanation: The olecranon is the curved projection at the back of the elbow where triceps inserts. The coronoid process and ulnar tuberosity receive brachialis.

Q: Which humeral depression accepts the olecranon when the forearm is fully extended?
A) Radial groove
B) Coronoid fossa
C) Olecranon fossa
D) Intertubercular groove
E) Radial fossa
Answer: C
Explanation: The olecranon fossa is posterior, above the trochlea. The coronoid fossa (anterior) receives the coronoid process in flexion and the radial fossa receives the radial head in full flexion.

Q: Which part of the distal humerus articulates with the head of the radius?
A) Coronoid fossa
B) Olecranon fossa
C) Trochlea
D) Medial epicondyle
E) Capitulum
Answer: E
Explanation: The capitulum is the lateral, globular articular surface that articulates with the radial head. The trochlea is spool-shaped and articulates with the trochlear notch of the ulna.

Q: A 24-year-old man punches a wall and has pain and a depressed knuckle on the ulnar side of his hand. A boxer's fracture of the metacarpal neck is suspected. Which bone is most typically involved in a boxer of lesser skill?

Explanation: Boxer's fracture involves metacarpal necks. It is typically the fifth metacarpal in less skilled boxers, whereas the second and third are involved in professional boxers.

Q: A Bennett fracture involves which part of the hand skeleton?

Q: A 68-year-old woman falls on her palm and presents with a dinner-fork deformity of the wrist. What is the most likely injury?

B) Boxer's fracture
C) Bennett fracture
D) Colles fracture
E) Smith fracture
Answer: D
Explanation: A Colles fracture is a distal radius fracture with posterior displacement or tilting of the distal fragment, giving a dinner (silver) fork deformity.

Q: An older patient falls onto the dorsum of the hand. Radiographs show a distal radial fracture with the fragment displaced anteriorly (garden spade deformity). What is this fracture?

Q: Which structures form the Guyon canal?

Q: A patient has pain, numbness, and tingling in the little finger and medial half of the ring finger, followed by weakness, and is treated by surgical decompression of a nerve at the wrist. Where is the nerve entrapped?

Q: Which joints share the common articular capsule of the elbow?

Q: The annular ligament attaches to which structure and encircles which structure?

Q: A 2-year-old girl cries and refuses to use her arm after being lifted by the wrist. Which underlying problem explains this injury?

Q: Pronation and supination occur at which joints?

Q: Which statement about the carrying angle is correct?
